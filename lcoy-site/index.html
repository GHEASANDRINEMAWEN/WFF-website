Live now: day 2 of the National LCOY in KigaliAction, policy and pitching. Youth innovators present their climate solutions.

From every hill to COP31.

LCOY Rwanda 2026 is the Local Conference of Youth on Climate Change. For five weeks, young Rwandans met in all 30 districts to set their climate priorities. In Kigali they turn them into a national youth position paper, carried to COP31 in Antalya.

Read the field reports See the programme

Three days in Kigali, 28–30 October

The National LCOY brings district and provincial priorities together, and ends with young people validating their position paper.

28Wednesday

Knowledge, dialogue and arts

Opening plenary with the Ministry of Environment, Rwanda Green Fund and youth advocates. World Café thematic labs, young leaders and nuclear energy sessions, a children and youth track for ages 6–17, and Arts for Climate performances.

29Thursday

Action, policy and pitching

A foresight workshop, Climate Innovation Pitching with 8–10 youth innovators, sessions on food systems, climate-resilient seed and biodiversity, the Policy Lab, and a closing plenary with live voting on the position paper.

30Friday

Culture, heritage and global solidarity

A cultural and heritage showcase, World Café on culture and climate, storytelling, music, poetry and visual arts, the Arts for Climate grand performance, and a mural unveiling.

Climate Month, step by step

26 September to 30 October 2026. Each stage feeds the next, from village tree planting to the national position paper.

  1. 26 September, nationwide

    Umuganda launch

    Climate Month opens on National Umuganda Day with community tree planting, including fruit trees, clean-ups, awareness campaigns and nutrition distribution in villages across the country.

    All 30 districts
  2. 27 September to 26 October, 30 districts

    District dialogues

    Youth dialogues in every district, many held in schools. Each produces a local priority list for the provincial consultations. Read the field reports

    2,100–3,000 young people
  3. 9–27 October, online and in Kigali

    Drafting the position paper

    A youth drafting team of 15–20 people turns every district and provincial input into the National Children and Youth Climate Position Paper.

  4. 20–23 October, 4 provinces

    Provincial consultations

    University-hosted gatherings carry rural priorities to the national level.

    • Eastern, 20 Oct, UR Nyagatare: climate-resilient agriculture and farmer-led solutions
    • Southern, 21 Oct, UR Huye: traditional knowledge and school outreach
    • Northern, 22 Oct, UR Musanze: agroecology and youth-led restoration
    • Western, 23 Oct, IPRC Karongi: Lake Kivu ecosystem, climate mobility and technology
    800–1,200 young people
  5. 28–30 October, Kigali

    National LCOY

    Three days of sessions, pitching and culture, closing with young people validating the position paper. See the three days

    600–800 young people

Why a youth conference on climate?

Young people are 65% of Rwanda's population, yet they have rarely had a structured, youth-led way to shape national climate decisions. The Local Conference of Youth changes that.

LCOYs are an official process of YOUNGO, the children and youth constituency of the UN climate convention. Since 2013, more than 50 countries have held one, feeding national priorities into the Global Youth Statement at each COP.

LCOY Rwanda 2026 produces a validated National Children and Youth Climate Position Paper, submitted to the Ministry of Environment and YOUNGO, and carried by a youth delegation to COP31 in Antalya, 9–20 November 2026.

  • Train young people in climate advocacy and UN climate processes3,500–5,000+ youth
  • Agree a national youth position paperTo YOUNGO and government
  • Connect youth networks across every provinceRwanda Youth Climate Coalition
  • Shape national policy, including the NDCs and NBSAPRecommendations tracked

Eight themes

Technology and digital climate action is a theme of its own and runs through all the others.

Climate-resilient agriculture and food systems

Climate-smart farming, agroecology, youth agripreneurship, nutrition and food security.

Biodiversity and ecosystem restoration

Nature-based solutions and forest landscape restoration, protecting Nyungwe, Volcanoes and Akagera.

Just energy transition and green jobs

Renewable energy access, the circular economy and routes into green work.

Loss and damage, climate mobility

Disaster risk reduction, climate finance, refugees, displaced people and forced migration.

Gender justice and disability inclusion

Women's climate leadership and climate policies that work for persons with disabilities.

Action for Climate Empowerment

Climate education, awareness, public participation and access to information.

Technology, innovation and digital action

AI, IoT, e-waste, biotechnology and digital tools for climate action.

Arts and culture for climate

Music, poetry, dance, drama, murals, waste-to-art, ceramics and storytelling.

Competitions across every theme

Young Rwandans compete with real climate solutions. The results feed the position paper and the Rwanda Youth Climate Coalition.
