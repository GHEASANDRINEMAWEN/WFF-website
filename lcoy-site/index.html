About the Conference

Youth voices, from the grassroots to COP31.

The Local Conference of Youth (LCOY) is an official youth-led process under YOUNGO. Since 2013, over 50 countries have hosted LCOYs, feeding national youth priorities into the Global Youth Statement presented at the annual UN climate conference (COP).

Rwanda's youth represent 65% of the population yet have historically lacked a structured, youth-led platform to shape national climate processes. LCOY Rwanda 2026 fills that gap — producing a validated National Children & Youth Climate Position Paper submitted to the Ministry of Environment and YOUNGO, and carried by a youth delegation to COP31 in Antalya, Turkey (Nov 9–20, 2026).

The conference is guided by six principles: youth-led, participatory, culturally grounded, inclusive by design, action-oriented, and grassroots-to-global.

Our goal: to empower young Rwandans to become active agents of climate action and influence national climate policy through a participatory, youth-led and inclusive conference process.

  1. Build youth capacity in climate advocacy and UNFCCC processes3,500–5,000+ youth trained
  2. Produce a validated National Children & Youth Climate Position PaperSubmitted to YOUNGO and Government
  3. Strengthen youth networks across all provincesPermanent Rwanda Youth Climate Coalition
  4. Influence national policy (NDCs, NBSAP)Position Paper recommendations tracked
  5. Showcase youth-led climate solutions8–10 innovations pitched at National LCOY
  • Approved by the LCOY Working Group under YOUNGO (UNFCCC)
  • Organised by 11 Technical Working Groups & 50+ youth leaders
  • Feeds into Rwanda's NDCs 3.0, NBSAP & the Global Youth Statement
Thematic Focus
Eight themes, one climate movement.
Technology & digital climate action is both a standalone theme and cross-cutting across every area.

Climate-Resilient Agriculture & Food Systems

Climate-smart practices, agroecology, youth agripreneurship, nutrition and food security.

Biodiversity Conservation & Ecosystem Restoration

Nature-based solutions, forest landscape restoration and the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework — protecting Nyungwe, Volcanoes and Akagera.

Just Energy Transition & Green Jobs

Renewable energy access, circular economy and green employment pathways.

Loss & Damage + Climate Mobility

Disaster risk reduction, climate finance, refugees, IDPs and forced migration.

Gender Justice, Disability Inclusion & Just Transition

Women's climate leadership, gender-responsive action and climate policies that are accessible and equitable for persons with disabilities.

Action for Climate Empowerment (ACE)

Climate education, awareness, public participation and access to information.

Technology, Innovation & Digital Climate Action

AI, IoT, e-waste management, biotechnology and digital tools for climate action.

Arts & Culture for Climate

Music, poetry, dance, drama, murals, waste-to-art, ceramics and storytelling for climate.

Thematic Competitions

From participation to solution-building.

Across all eight themes, young Rwandans compete with real climate solutions. Competition outputs feed directly into the National Children & Youth Climate Position Paper and the Rwanda Youth Climate Coalition.

The Climate Month
Five phases, one journey.
September 26 – October 30, 2026. From National Umuganda Day to the National LCOY Main Event in Kigali.
September 26 · Nationwide

Umuganda Launch

Climate Month launches on National Umuganda Day with community tree planting (including fruit trees), clean-ups, climate awareness campaigns and nutrition distribution across all villages.

All 30 districts
September 27 – October 26 · 30 Districts

District Dialogues

30 district-level youth dialogues run continuously through the month. Each produces a local priority list feeding provincial consultations and the National LCOY.

2,100–3,000 youth
October 19–23 · 4 Provinces

Provincial Consultations

University-hosted gatherings elevate rural youth priorities to the national level.

  • Eastern · Oct 20 · UR Nyagatare — Climate-Resilient Agriculture & Farmer-Led Solutions
  • Southern · Oct 21 · UR Huye — Traditional Knowledge & School Outreach
  • Northern · Oct 22 · UR Musanze — Agroecology & Youth-Led Restoration
  • Western · Oct 23 · IPRC Karongi — Lake Kivu Ecosystem, Climate Mobility & Technology
800–1,200 youth
October 9–27 · Virtual + Kigali

Statement Consolidation

A youth drafting team consolidates all inputs into the National Children & Youth Climate Position Paper.

15–20 drafters
October 28–30 · Kigali

National LCOY Main Event

Three days in Kigali, closing with the validation of the National Children & Youth Climate Position Paper.

  • Day 1 · Oct 28 — Knowledge, Dialogue & Arts: opening plenary with the Ministry of Environment, Rwanda Green Fund and youth advocates; World Café thematic labs; Young Leaders and Nuclear Energy sessions; a Children & Youth Track (ages 6–17); Arts for Climate performances.
  • Day 2 · Oct 29 — Action, Policy & Pitching: foresight workshop; Climate Innovation Pitching with 8–10 youth innovators; food systems, climate-resilient seed and biodiversity sessions; the Policy Lab; closing plenary with live voting on the Position Paper.
  • Day 3 · Oct 30 — Culture, Heritage & Global Solidarity: cultural & heritage showcase; World Café on culture and climate; storytelling, music, poetry and visual arts sessions; Arts for Climate Grand Performance; mural unveiling.
600–800 youth

Host Sessions & Side Events

Lead a thematic session, workshop, panel or side event at the National LCOY Main Event in Kigali — or run an event in your province or district during Climate Month.

  • National LCOY sessions — Kigali, Oct 28–30. A 45–90 minute thematic session, workshop or panel.
  • National side events — exhibitions, workshops and networking running parallel to main sessions.
  • Provincial events — Eastern, Southern, Northern & Western Provinces, Oct 19–23.
  • District activities — any of the 30 districts, Sept 27 – Oct 26.
Applications closed

Schools & Higher Learning Institutions

Primary schools, secondary schools and universities can host climate activities on their own campus during Climate Month, with trained LCOY delegates sent to facilitate.

  • School-based activities — climate club sessions, tree planting, art competitions and awareness events.
  • Higher learning institutions — climate dialogues, research showcases and innovation events on campus.
  • LCOY delegates facilitate on site, bringing a youth-led approach to your institution.
  • Competitions encouraged — art, essays, debates, school gardens, recycling and innovation.
