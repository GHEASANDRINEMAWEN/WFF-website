About the Conference

Youth voices, from the grassroots to COP31.

The Local Conference of Youth (LCOY) is an official youth-led process under YOUNGO. Since 2013, over 50 countries have hosted LCOYs, feeding national youth priorities into the Global Youth Statement presented at the annual UN climate conference (COP).

Rwanda's youth represent 65% of the population yet have historically lacked a structured, youth-led platform to shape national climate processes. LCOY Rwanda 2026 fills that gap — producing a validated National Children & Youth Climate Position Paper submitted to the Ministry of Environment and YOUNGO, and carried by a youth delegation to COP31 in Antalya, Turkey (Nov 9–20, 2026).

The conference is guided by six principles: youth-led, participatory, culturally grounded, inclusive by design, action-oriented, and grassroots-to-global.

  • Approved by the LCOY Working Group under YOUNGO (UNFCCC)
  • Organised by 11 Technical Working Groups & 50+ youth leaders
  • Feeds into Rwanda's NDCs 3.0, NBSAP & the Global Youth Statement
Thematic Focus
Eight themes, one climate movement.
Technology & digital climate action is both a standalone theme and cross-cutting across every area.

Climate-Resilient Agriculture & Food Systems

Climate-smart practices, agroecology, youth agripreneurship, nutrition and food security.

Biodiversity & Ecosystem Restoration

Nature-based solutions and forest landscape restoration across Rwanda's national parks.

Just Energy Transition & Green Jobs

Renewable energy access, circular economy and green employment pathways.

Loss & Damage + Climate Mobility

Disaster risk reduction, climate finance, refugees, IDPs and forced migration.

Gender Justice & Just Transition

Women's climate leadership, empowerment and gender-responsive action.

Action for Climate Empowerment (ACE)

Climate education, awareness, public participation and access to information.

Technology, Innovation & Digital Climate Action

AI, IoT, e-waste management and digital tools for climate action and advocacy.

Arts & Culture for Climate

Music, poetry, dance, drama, murals, waste-to-art and storytelling for climate.

The Climate Month
Five phases, one journey.
September 26 – October 29, 2026. From National Umuganda Day to the National LCOY Main Event in Kigali.
September 26 · Nationwide

Umuganda Launch

Climate Month launches on National Umuganda Day with community tree planting (including fruit trees), clean-ups, climate awareness campaigns and nutrition distribution across all villages.

All 30 districts
September 27 – October 26 · 30 Districts

District Dialogues

30 district-level youth dialogues run continuously through the month. Each produces a local priority list feeding provincial consultations and the National LCOY.

1,500–3,000 youth
October 5–8 · 4 Provinces

Provincial Consultations

University-hosted gatherings at UR Nyagatare, UR Huye, UR Musanze and IPRC Karongi elevate rural youth priorities to the national level.

800–1,200 youth
October 9–27 · Virtual + Kigali

Statement Consolidation

A youth drafting team consolidates all inputs into the National Children & Youth Climate Position Paper.

15–20 drafters
October 28–29 · Kigali

National LCOY Main Event

A 2-day conference: high-level panels, World Café thematic labs, a youth innovation showcase & pitching event, arts performances, a policy lab and a validation plenary. Closes at 15:00 on Oct 29.

600–800 youth
